## Feature assortment

- **Usefulness rankings** for kanji and words, used to order study [1].
- **Drawing practice** for kana and kanji components [1].
- **SRS flashcards** with automatic progress tracking, import and export [1].
- **Games:** Kanji Challenge, Quiz, Match, Builder, Draw, Keywords, Pop; Word Quiz, Match, Sentence Builder, Complete; Grammar Match [1].
- **AI TeachMe and AI Sentence Correct** [1].
- **Graded reader,** "Joy o' Kanji" essays, and a mastery scale (0–4) per item, aligned to JLPT [1].

## Strengths

- **Usefulness ordering answers "what next?"** without forcing a fixed path.
- **Earn Pro by studying.** Rewarding effort with access is a clever retention loop.
- **Many short game formats** give variety on low-energy days.
- **AI correction of the learner's own sentences** covers output practice.

## Weaknesses

- **Breadth over polish.** Dozens of modes split attention (analysis).
- **Monthly caps** on the free plan make the boundary feel arbitrary.

## Lessons for Japanese VMA

- **Order by usefulness.** Japanese VMA's `frequency` column on words (from the dictionary import) and JLPT levels can drive a "most useful unknown words in your lists" sort. Sortable dictionary columns (#426) are a stepping stone.
- **A mastery scale beats a boolean.** The Known* catalogues are binary. A four-step mastery level from flashcard results (new → seen → learning → known) is more honest and can feed every other feature.
- **Earn-by-studying** could fit the membership spec as a later experiment. It is a monetisation choice, so it belongs to the owner.
- **AI sentence correction** is a natural tool for the planned chat assistant.
