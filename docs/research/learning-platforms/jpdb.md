## Feature assortment

- **Media decks.** Learn the vocabulary of a specific show or novel, in frequency order [1].
- **Kanji taught alongside vocabulary,** with Heisig-style keywords that jpdb says it hand-curated to be "better" [1].
- **Text extraction.** Paste text and the words are identified and organised [1].
- **Automatic i+1 sentence cards.** The target word appears in an example sentence where every other word is already known [1].
- **Media recommendations** based on the learner's vocabulary level [1].
- **Anki import,** progress across decks, no daily card limit [1].

## Strengths

- **i+1 is the right difficulty.** A sentence with exactly one unknown word is understandable and still teaches something.
- **Goal-oriented decks.** "I want to watch X" is a far stronger motivator than "learn N4".
- **Low switching cost.** Anki import lets learners bring their history.

## Weaknesses

- **Mostly vocabulary.** Grammar and listening are out of scope.
- **The deck catalogue reflects anime and VN culture.** That is great for that audience and less useful for business or travel learners (analysis).
- **Machine-learned scheduling is opaque.** Learners cannot easily see why an item is due.

## Lessons for Japanese VMA

- **Coverage and difficulty per article.** Japanese VMA already knows each article's words. Combined with the learner's Known words, it can show "you know 82% of this article" and recommend articles to read next. This is jpdb's media recommendation applied to Japanese VMA's articles.
- **i+1 sentence selection.** Japanese VMA stores Tatoeba sentences linked to words. Choosing a sentence whose other words are all Known gives i+1 cloze cards without any new data.
- **"Learn this article's words" decks.** A one-click catalogue built from an article's unknown words, then studied in flashcards.
- **Import known words** (pasted text or an Anki export) to seed the Known* catalogues. Without this, coverage features are useless for existing learners.
