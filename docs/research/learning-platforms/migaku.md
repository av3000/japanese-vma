## Feature assortment

- **Works on Netflix, YouTube and general web pages,** including imported subtitles [1].
- **Words coloured as known, learning or unknown** in any text, with a comprehension score per piece of content [1].
- **One-click card creation** from subtitles or text. Cards carry the sentence, audio, screenshot, definition and an AI explanation, and whole dialogues can become cards [1].
- **Pitch accent information,** monolingual dictionaries, ChatGPT explanations, text-to-speech [1].
- **Import words you already know** [1].

## Strengths

- **Study inside the content the learner already wants to consume.** The motivation is intrinsic.
- **The comprehension score** answers "is this show too hard for me?" before the learner commits.
- **Rich cards made with one click.** Sentence mining stops being a chore.

## Weaknesses

- **Depends on third-party sites.** Platform changes (DRM, DOM changes) can break features (analysis).
- **No structured teaching.** It assumes the learner can already follow some content.
- **Promotional pricing** makes the real price unclear.

## Lessons for Japanese VMA

- **Known/unknown colouring in the article reader** is the highest-impact idea for Japanese VMA's core use case: reading articles. Japanese VMA has the article's word list and the learner's Known words, so it needs only text positions and a style.
- **A comprehension score on article cards** ("82% known") turns the article list into a reading recommender.
- **One-click mining from the reader.** Click a word to add it, with its sentence, to a chosen catalogue. The add-to-catalogue widget exists; capturing the sentence is new.
- **Pitch accent** is a later option and needs a licensed data source. Treat it as an open question.
