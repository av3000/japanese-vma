## Feature assortment

- **A guided map** of themed regions from beginner to N1 [1].
- **Grammar lessons with SRS exercises,** multiple review styles, and "automatic homework based on what you just learned" [1].
- **Kanji with mnemonics** that use other kanji as building blocks [1].
- **Study lists,** personal or from "thousands of lists based on your favourite anime, games, novels, or manga" [1].
- **Readings with audio and translations** [1].
- **Heatmaps, progress bars, graphs, achievements, badges and titles** [1].

## Strengths

- **One path for everything.** Grammar, kanji and vocabulary are interleaved, so learners do not stitch three apps together.
- **"Homework" right after a lesson** turns the lesson into immediate retrieval practice.
- **"No AI" is a trust signal** in a market crowded with generated content.

## Weaknesses

- **The most expensive lifetime tier audited** (USD 349.99).
- **The game framing** may not suit adult or business learners (analysis).

## Lessons for Japanese VMA

- **Practise right after reading.** After finishing an article, offer "Practise this article's new words now". The flashcards route already accepts a catalogue, so a temporary deck from an article is the missing piece.
- **Provenance labels matter.** If Japanese VMA uses AI (chat assistant) alongside human-written articles and imported NHK news, label each source clearly. The IMPORT-07 provenance badge (#411) is the right place to start.
